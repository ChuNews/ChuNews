Katy (2 of 4)

Mark Chu November 7, 2026

Short Story - Horror

Chapter 2

George Burgess was twenty-seven. Young, rich, and reckless. He had inherited a big fortune and indulged himself with fine wine, gourmet food, and young ladies. He was a man with words sweeter than honey. Many innocent hearts were lifted and then broken.

Then he met Katy.

Katy’s father, Dr. Richmond, was a well-respected physician in town. George befriended Dr. Richmond at a social gathering. They sometimes went hunting or fishing together. The friendship was not exactly mutual, however. Dr. Richmond was glad to have a rich friend to boost his reputation. George, on the other hand, wasn’t truly fond of a fifty-two-year-old man’s company. It was merely a good excuse to stay at Dr. Richmond’s house to see his daughter.

Katy was twenty-one years old. She always wore a black snood around her neck. A blonde knockout, she liked to sit under a big oak tree near a hilltop. She would bring a simple lunch or snack, read, paint, enjoy the scenery, and daydream. But when necessary, she could be as fierce and sharp like a fighting knife, and that created tension between her and her father like an old string stretched way too thin, ready to break.

On a particular day, Katy was in the front yard writing. She was so immersed in her work that she didn’t hear her father calling. When he stood right in front of her, she didn’t even lift her eyebrows.

“Katy!” he shouted for the hundredth time.

She was startled. “What, Father?”

“I called you a million times! Why did you ignore me?!” His face was bright red.

“No, sorry, I didn’t ignore…”

“Why are you idling like this? How many times have I told you? You should be practicing your sewing!”

Katy pressed her lips together. She looked down and started writing again.

“Look at me when I’m talking to you!”

She didn’t move.

“I said look at me!”

Still looking down, she kept writing without showing any sign of stopping.

He grabbed the pen from her hand and threw it away into the bushes.

Katy quietly stood up, walked to the bushes, found the pen, walked back, sat down, and kept writing.

When George first saw Katy, he carefully studied her face and every emotion expressed by it. She was different from any other woman he had known. She was beautiful, but seemed not to know or care about her own beauty. She seemed vulnerable, but even the whole world would not change her mind. She presented as a challenge to George. A fascinating adventure.

George first used jewelry, but soon he found she couldn’t care less. He lavished praise and compliments on her. He boasted of his wealth and achievements. He showered her with gifts. When he realized all these strategies, which always worked on other women with one-tenth of the effort, fell short like a deflated ball in December’s cold air, he knew he had to do something drastically different.

He learned that she loved stories. He asked a writer friend to write one, then read it to Katy as if he had written it.

It worked. She was hooked. She wanted more, and her admiration toward him grew like a budding flower slowly blossoming.

He read her more stories. He stayed later and later in her room.

Finally, one night, he asked her. She hesitated. But he wouldn’t give up. Come, my love, don’t be afraid. I’ll take care of you, for the rest of my life, I’ll love you forever…no, it wouldn’t hurt, I promise… I’ll take full responsibility, I’ll marry you, I’ll love you…

...

She was pregnant.

The rage and humiliation. They spread like wildfire. As a well-respected doctor in town, Dr. Richmond and his esteemed wife surely didn’t deserve this embarrassment. Unless they got married right away to cover it up, he would have this daughter no more.

But George stopped coming to visit. He was always out of town or busy.

After so many failed attempts by the doctor and Katy to get hold of him, she went straight to George’s house herself.

She was stopped at the gate and waited for nearly an hour in the cold February wind. When she finally got into the property, she was coldly greeted by George’s mother, Madam Eleanor Burgess, who implied that she saw Katy as nothing more than another girl who was scheming to get her hands on George and his inheritance. That would never happen, she made it very clear to Katy. “Do not ever come back. I never want to see your face. Ever.”

Katy ran out of the gate and sat on a rock with her head in her hands crying. She vowed to herself never to step on their property ever again.

Suddenly, she heard a cough, then a soft voice asked, “What seems to be the problem?”

She turned toward the source of the voice. A young man sitting on a rock not far away from her was looking at her.

She wiped the tears from her face and stood up. “Pardon me.” She turned to leave.

“Is it my cousin? George?”

She stopped and looked at him.

“Norman.”

“…Katy. You… do you know where he is? Would you please…”

“I don’t know.” He shook his head. “I truly wish I knew. I’m sorry.”

She sat down. Tears started pouring down her face again.

Norman sighed. “I’m so sorry. I told him so many times not to do such horrible, careless things, hurting innocent people, driving so many lives to the brink of insanity…” He started coughing. Violently.

“Are you alright? My father is a doctor…”

He forced a smile while catching his breath. “I’ll tell him to come talk to you. We’re very close, he might listen.”

“Thank you. Thank you, sir… I haven’t seen a smile for quite a long time, I appreciate such warm treatment and quite enjoy it…”

“Such a shame. A beautiful young woman like you should be greeted with many smiles every day. Ah, look.” His attention was drawn to something high up in the trees. Katy looked up.

A blue jay. With crystal green wings.

Norman smiled. “Beautiful birds. One in a million.” He closed his eyes. “I imagine I can fly like one.”

“Every day I sit under an oak tree and imagine flying with them.”

“Oh, I’d love that…”

But then he had a coughing fit again. He covered his mouth; blood poured out from between his fingers. He collapsed. Katy ran to his side and helped him up. “Help! Help!” She broke her own vow by struggling to get him back to the gate and fiercely pounding on it.

Madam Burgess walked out to check on the noise. “What’s the matter? All these commotions… you! Why are you still here?” Seeing all the blood on Norman, she yelled, “My Norman! What did you do to him, you little witch!?”

“Please help! He’s coughing blood!” Katy screamed.

Some servants rushed to open the gate. After lots of screams and shouts, they carried him back into the mansion. Madam Burgess turned back and stared at Katy, then hurried back into the house. Katy stood alone in the cold wind watching them close the door.
