Katy (3 of 4)

Mark Chu November 7, 2026

Short Story - Horror

Chapter 3

Dr. Richmond disowned Katy and chased her out of the house.

“Please, Father, where would I go?”

“You bring shame to this family. I don’t have a daughter. Get out.”

Katy first relied on the mercy of several family friends, but soon they turned their backs on her. She was forced to sell everything she had to rent a room in a run-down area. She would go for days living solely on several pieces of bread. A shower and clean clothes on a freezing day became luxuries too costly to afford.

Katy knew she had only one choice. She had to do it quickly.

As she entered the tiny and dark room, she thought she smelled rotten eggs. She was ordered to lie down and open her legs. She shivered uncontrollably, and her heart pounded like it would burst at any second. God, my Lord, where are you? I’m so lonely, have mercy on me…    

Norman’s smile came to her mind. She closed her eyes. A smile will be all I need. I’ll die for just a smile.

I’ll die for just a smile.

Two days later.

“Norman my dear, where are you going? You should rest.” Madam Burgess saw Norman walking out the door.

“Good evening, my dear aunt. Someone wants to see me, I’ll be right back.”

“Who?” She looked out of the window. A shadow was standing at the front gate. “That woman again? What are you meeting her for?” Madam Burgess’s reached out to grab him. “My beloved child, you can’t possibly think meeting this little witch is worth your time?”

“Please, Aunt Burgess. She saved my life for heaven’s sake. She’s my friend.” He walked out.

Madam Burgess saw Norman open the gate and greet Katy. They both walked into the woods.

“Katy, you look beautiful... just a little pale,” Norman said. “By the way, I got hold of George and urged him to come back. I didn’t tell him what for, so he must think it was about me. He should be here soon.”

“Some have to face their wrongdoing for the rest of their lives. But I’m not here for George.”

“No?”

“Please forgive my foolish impulse. I just need someone to talk to. I hope I didn’t scare or disgust you.”

“Oh no, not at all! Why would I be? Please. I long to have the privilege of enjoying your company once more. I haven’t thanked you for saving me, even though you were in such turmoil yourself...”

“Who wouldn’t? But I thank you for your compliment.”  

“What should we talk about, then? If I can be of any help please do tell.”

“I miss your smile.”

He smiled. “Katy, my days are numbered, despite what my aunt and George are so eager to believe. But if you don’t get tired of it, I can give you a smile every day as long as I live.”

“Such a nice gift. But if you know your days are numbered, aren’t you afraid?”

He paused for a moment, then shook his head. “No. I’m finally at peace. Only when one knows one’s end is near will one cherish every moment.”

Katy quietly looked at him. “You’re so different from him.”

“But we are closer than brothers.”

“May I inquire why?”

He pressed his lips together. “My father used to visit my aunt often.”

She slowly nodded. “How close are you?”

“He trusts me with his most precious treasure.” He took out a golden pocket watch with small shining diamonds on it. “It belonged to his late Father. He passed away when George was two months shy of six. Now whenever he goes traveling, he’ll leave it to me so he won’t lose it somewhere.” He flipped it open and showed her the time. “Don’t you think time is such a particular, funny thing? We live as if we have an endless supply of it. And yet it still treats everyone equally.”

She walked close to him and said softly, “What if time forever stands still, but life has a new beginning?”

A little surprised, he locked eyes with her. Though he felt a chill on his skin, her eyes warmed him in the chest. A gradual realization dawned on his face. He smiled.

George came back from his leisure trip overseas. It was such a splendid cruise; he enjoyed so much fresh air, marvelous food and wine, and shared a lot of laughs and intimate moments with so many women. It was another conquest. Better yet, he saw two women fight for him, physically; they almost tore each other’s hair and clothes. He laughed. Of course, he heard that one girl tried to commit suicide. Or was it two girls? He couldn’t remember. They were simply too dramatic anyway; too emotionally immature. During the trip, he caught himself whispering into a mirror with a grin more than once, “I feel like the king of the world… I am.”

Upon seeing him, however, his mother was puzzled by his paleness. “What’s wrong?”

“Nothing, mother.”

“Nothing?”

“Really.”

“Then there’s something for you to…”

“I’m tired, mother. I should talk to you in the morning.”

She stared at him sternly with disapproval. “Right. Have you talked to Norman?”

“Ah, yes, Norman. No, I haven’t. How’s he doing? I came back because of him. He said he had such an emergency he had to see me right away. I’m worried about him.”

“He’s alright, but his condition is extremely unstable, just the other day… anyway, you should talk to him.”

“Yes, mother.” He leaned forward and gave her a kiss. “Where is he?”

“He should be back in his room resting already.”

George walked to his room. “Norman, so what’s the emergency… Norman?”

Norman wasn’t in the room. He frowned. A servant happened to walk by. “Where’s my cousin?” he asked.

“I’m…not sure, sir. He and a lady have been in the woods for a while.”

“A lady?”

“Yes, sir. The lady that came to look for you the other day.”

“What did you say?”

“The lady came to look for you the other day.”

“Wh… What’s her name?”

“I don’t know, sir.”

George walked hastily to his mother’s room. “Mother? Mother? Where’s Norman?”

Madam Burgess sat in a big chair with a cup of tea in her hand. “What’s the rush? I told you he should be back in his room.”

“He’s not. A servant told me he was with a lady?”

“What? He’s still with that woman?”

“What woman?”

“Your trouble, that’s what.” She stared at him. “You reckon the name Katy Richmond?”

George firmly shook his head. “Impossible, you’re mistaken.”

“What’s that supposed to mean, George Burgess?”

“It can’t be her. You’re mistaken.”

“I know who I saw, son.”

“I’m telling you that you’re wrong!” He yelled.

“How dare you!” She put down the cup and stood up. “That tone! And what on earth makes you so sure that I’m wrong?”

“Because she’s dead! She died two days ago!”

“What?” She gasped.

“I saw her body with my own eyes! She was soaked in blood…”

“How did you…”

“I was on my way home late last night, but I ran into the Police Chief. We talked for a while, then he said they found a body in a makeshift operation room. I… I went with him. She…” He raised his trembling hands. “…she lay in a tiny metal bed, blood everywhere…” He then buried his face in his hands. “Then the Police Chief said something out of the blue, ‘Some have to face their wrongdoing for the rest of their lives.’ I… I think he was talking to me, but I don’t know what he meant by that.”

Madam Burgess walked straight out of the door, and George followed. “Norman!” “Norman!” they both shouted, ordering servants to join the search. Everyone spread out. “Norman!” George and his mother went into the woods.

Suddenly, they heard a female servant’s horrified shriek. They rushed toward it.

Katy’s and Norman’s bodies lay on the ground next to a big fallen tree trunk. In her hand was the gold pocket watch.
