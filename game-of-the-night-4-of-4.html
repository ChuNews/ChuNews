Game of the Night (4 of 4)

Mark Chu October 18, 2026

Short Story - Fantasy / Horror

Chapter 4

Looking at Mrs. Raven’s dead body, Mr. Wolfe scratched his head and murmured. “Okay, I might be wrong.”

“Well, she did that to herself,” people standing around said.

“Yeah. I’ll bury her.” Mr. Wolfe carried her body into the woods.

The day after Mrs. Raven was murdered, thirteen bodies were found; an unprecedented number. What was more disturbing was that they were arranged in a circle like a ritual.

Mr. Goad and Mr. Wolfe sent more people to the guillotine. Fifteen. Twenty the next day. And yet more and more bodies were found the next morning.

Mrs. Fowler was found floating in the river. “A faithful Christian like her… God’s wrath…” Mr. Goad almost lost his mind. He ordered all the men who had participated in the water test to be executed.

“Mr. Goad, not many people left. We’ll have to send mothers or their children to the machine if we want more,” Mr. Wolfe said. “And… Maybe that’s indeed not a good idea. I mean… Maybe… maybe she was right.”

“Maybe… But if it’s necessary…”

“Mrs. Donker is still finding more names.”

Mr. Goad buried his head in his hands. “This demon is… different.”

“It is. Mr. Goad, I suggest you carry your cross all the time. As a Holy Man, a prophet, the regular demon might not be able to go near you. But I have a feeling this one might be stronger just a bit.”

“You might be right. You might just be right.”

Mr. Wolfe gulped, trembling a little. “Mr. Goad, I just realized something.”

“What thing?”

“All… all the people either killed by this monster or executed are…”

“What? Speak.”

“…are those who supported the decision six years ago. Is it a coincidence… or a punishment?”

“Nonsense.” Mr. Goad’s voice was weak. “That’s nonsense.”

“Okay. Mr. Goad, I’ll go home now. You take care.”

* * *

In bed, Mr. Goad held the cross tightly. He was very tired but too anxious to fall asleep, like a windmill that wanted to stop but the strong wind kept spinning it. Exhaustion would overcome him every five to ten seconds, but then he would jump and hold the cross even tighter.    

“Tomorrow, we start with the five mothers and their ten kids at the eastern part of the village. I’ll find the demon. I’ll keep my reputation.”

Suddenly, something blew out the candle. It was pitch black. “Ah!” He was shaking.

Absolute silence.

He saw a shadow slowly moving in the room. It split into dozens of shadows and started dancing and singing. Some had blurry faces, and they were smiling. He recognized a few of them. The bigger they smiled, the more horrified he was. Behind all the shadows there was a fire. It was just a dim light in the beginning but grew brighter and brighter, casting more shadows from the shadows.

He closed his eyes and raised the cross up high, screaming at the top of his lungs, “Begone! Devil! Begone!”

When he opened his eyes, the cross turned into a piece of wood with thick brown stains. It was from the guillotine. He shrieked and threw it away.   

All went dark. Silence again.

A black cat appeared at the window. It lightly and elegantly jumped to the end of the bed. The moment she touched the bed, she turned into Mrs. Raven.

“No,” he said, shaking his head.

“I’m not here to judge,” she said.

“What?”

Then he was on the guillotine, looking at Mr. Goad, who was smiling. He realized he was all the countless people he himself had ordered to be imprisoned or executed, all at once. He was the teen girl, separated from her parents and family, terrified and crying, thrown into solitary confinement. “No!” He tried but couldn’t break free. Suddenly he was deep in the water. Looking up, he saw his reflection, but it was Mrs. Raven’s despairing face.

Slowly, his eyes finally closed.   

* * *

The news of Mr. Goad’s death spread like plague. Mrs. Donker screamed and screamed, tearing her clothes and pulling her hair out. She went insane, walking half-naked from then on.

With no one left to order executions, the village became dead quiet. Those who survived, mostly women and children, scrambled to make ends meet. The skilled women gathered what was left: livestock, crops, tools, and anything available to rebuild the village.

* * *

A month after the death of Mr. Goad.

It was a sunny but somewhat chilly afternoon. Mr. Wolfe walked up a tall hill, looking over the endless forest.

A shadow approached him. When it was right behind him, he smiled without turning around. “Raven.”

“Wolf.” Raven came to stand right next to him, looking over the forest with him.

They were quiet for a long while. The wind started to pick up. Wolf squinted, feeling the gusts on his face. Raven raised her head high and extended her arms like flying.

“Do you smell that?” Wolf said, smiling.

“Yeah?”

“Grass. The mud around the lake. The oaks and birches. No blood.”

“No blood. We gave them so many chances, didn’t we?”

“That we did. You did. I just made sure innocent blood was avenged.”

Another quiet moment.  

“Was it a little close? Just a little?” Raven said. “They knew the only way to kill us.”

“We’ve been doing this for thousands of years.”

She looked at him.

“Then it’ll be just five hundred years of sleep. A blink of an eye.”

“You don’t know that. No one has been resurrected.”

“Yet.”

She smirked, then whispered, “Death is a little long for me.”

He locked eyes with her, his hand resting on her arm. “Not in the eye of eternity.”

She touched his back. He smiled at her. “It’s nothing to be afraid of. I’ve heard it’s beautiful; so beautiful, the problem is you might not want to come back. And it’s another beginning. Life never ends, even for us.”

She slowly exhaled.

“Anyhow, it’s all over, Raven. Let’s keep feeling the sun and the wind.”

“Don’t you see the sky? It’s so blue today.”

They closed their eyes and felt the wind blowing past their torsos.

Wolf said, “Plus, if something really happens to us, someone will continue the work.”

Another shadow walked up the hill.

“Speaking of the devil. Or monster. Mr. Owler,” Raven greeted. “So glad to see you. Welcome.”

Mr. Owler’s eyes flashed red for a split second. His tongue ran over his fangs for a while before he asked, puzzled, “What… what’s all this? Why… What am I…?”

Wolf and Raven exchanged a look and smiled.

“Next?” Wolf said.

“There.” Raven eyed another village beyond the forest.

“What’s the situation?”

“Fear, as usual. Not as much killing, but you’ll need to go under this time.”

Wolf and Raven walked down the hill. Mr. Owler hesitated for a moment, then followed along.

- The End -
