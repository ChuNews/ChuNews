The Old, Old Song "To Know Him is to Love Him"--and the Attachment Style

Mark Chu August 29, 2026

I'd want to talk about this old song, To Know Him is to Love Him (1958) by The Teddy Bears. 

Here are the lyrics:

To know, know know him, is to love, love, love him

Just to see him smile, makes my life worthwhile

To know, know know him, is to love, love, love him

And I do

I'll be good to him, I'll bring love to him

Everyone says there'll come a day when I'll walk alongside of him

Yes, yes to know him, is to love, love, love him

And I do, I do

(Bridge)

Why can't he see?  

How blind can he be?

Someday he will see

That he was meant for me, oh oh, yes (back to first stanza)

Whenever I listen to this song, I feel a longing of a thousand years for the love I can't never receive; a one-sided, unrequited love.  Relating it to psychological science (sorry, career hazard), this reminds me of the preoccupied romantic attachment style.  Which I've mentioned before.

This is the style when an individual is obsessed with the love interest, puts them on a pedestal, and feels the pain of a thousand fires in every single second for not being able to be with them.  

"Just to see him smile makes my life worthwhile." This statement shows how much she's willing to sacrifice, even dignity, just to get his attention.  In the heat of the passion, a preoccupied individual might even be willing to offer their own lives to their love, despite not knowing them well.  You might think that's ridiculous, but if you understand that their whole identity and self-worth depend on being loved by that particular person, it isn't that crazy anymore.

Preoccupied individuals tend to see others, especially the romantic interest, as the "better" ones, and at the same time see themselves as potentially not worthy.  To prove their worth, they have to have the love they long for.  They'll go to any lengths to get it.

"Everyone says there'll come a day when I'll walk alongside of him." If you take it on the surface value, you might think, "So maybe there are some mutual feelings, otherwise others won't see them as a potential couple." But keep in mind who the narrator is.  

On the other hand, however, this might not be said by a typical preoccupied individual. It has a little sense of entitlement, and preoccupied people are usually less likely to have that.   

This becomes more obvious in the bridge when she starts blaming him for being "so blind." "Why can't you understand?  We're meant to be together!"

A preoccupied individual's heart, at least in the beginning of the relationship when it's full of uncertainties, will scream, "I know I don't deserve your love, but please love me." Instead, this blaming game reminds me of borderline personality.  You know, the movie Fatal Attraction.  The song isn't so extreme, but the idea is a bit like the difference between "Please love me, or I'll die" vs. "Love me, or else."

That said, the whole song still gives me a more preoccupied feel.  Besides what we've mentioned, "To know him is to love him," "I'll be good to him, I'll bring love to him" are all telltale signs as well; the fire that burns so quick and bright, like the world can collapse now and I won't give a dime.  Please love me.

Is this healthy?  Probably not.  Research shows preoccupied individuals are more likely to break up, mostly due to their intensive responses to any possible threats to the relationship ("Are you interested in that man?" "Are you flirting with that woman?").  Yet I find lots of music, media, and other platforms are pushing people in that direction.  They normalize, even glorify, unhealthy styles of relationships.
