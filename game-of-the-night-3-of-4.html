Game of the Night (3 of 4)

Mark Chu October 18, 2026

Short Story - Fantasy / Horror

Chapter 3

More heads were cut off, and more people died during the night. Now every morning there would be four or five bodies. No one cared for the fields; the sheep, cows, and horses were running around. Stores were closed.

Except for supervising the beheadings, Mr. Goad now locked himself in his room, murmuring all day while holding a cross against his chest. Mrs. Donker called out names, and most of the names would soon be without heads.

One day, Mr. Goad walked out of his room, watching the bloody head-cutting machine without a word.

“Mr. Goad,” Mr. Wolfe walked by, took off his hat to greet him, then kept walking.

“Mr. Wolfe,” Mr. Goad called.

“Yes, sir?”

“Mr. Doggs mentioned that you both saw the devil?”

“Sure did. We were lucky to be alive.”

“And it looked more like a… monster? Not a spirit?”

“Indeed.”

Mr. Goad sighed. “You mentioned the shape-shifter? A skinwalker?”

Mr. Wolfe lowered his head with an uneasy smile.

“No no, I grant you permission to speak. Loosen your tongue.”   

“Really?” Mr. Wolfe’s eyes sparkled. “You would listen?”

“As true as I’m the mouthpiece of the Lord.”

“Okay! Okay! Where do I start? Yes, my old mama, God rest her soul, taught me about the shape-shifters and skinwalkers when I was a kid… They ain’t afraid of daylight. My old mama loved cooking, right? And at dinner every day, she would prepare…”

“Skip the mama part, please.”

“Oh, but it’s related. My old mama would prepare three dishes, a lot of corn, and so she’d go to the field, and…” Looking at Mr. Goad’s face, he said, “…and the shape-shifters like to change their forms; well, obviously, as their name indicates, haha. They like to live among people, you know? They kill for energy, but also absorb it from sunlight. Oh, and they run lightning fast like hell! In the blink of an eye they’re in front of you now, heya!” He jumped. “Now, how do they kill, you ask? Well, they do have fangs like vampires, but shorter. But usually they just shock the victims, and they die in a second. Foolproof.”

“That’s why no blood, no injury.”

“That’s why. Now, how to kill them, you ask. The best way is to drown them, then bury them deep in…”

“No no, Mr. Wolfe, you’re misinformed. I know little about monsters of this kind, but one thing I’m certain of is how to kill one…” He stroked his goatee. “Decapitation is the only way.”

“Oh.” Mr. Wolfe raised an eyebrow, then pulled his hat down and lowered his head.

“So we’re doing the right thing, just need to find the right person.”

“I see, Holy Man.”

“How about skinwalkers?”

“They’re pretty much the same, except they fly. Oh, both shape-shifters and skinwalkers have red eyes.”

“I see. Thank you, Mr. Wolfe. Valuable information. Now the question is which demon we’re dealing with right now. How do we know?”

Mr. Wolfe scratched his head. “Mama didn’t say… After all, they all change shapes. They can be human now, the next minute a bird, then a cat…”

“We’ll have to cast a bigger net then.”

“Of course. So, what are you going to do? I’d like to help if that suits you.”

Mr. Goad closed his eyes and prayed, “Oh Lord, show me thy way. You have shown me who to trust and who to punish before, who to love and who to hate, who to blame and put away. Now give me the wisdom again, I beg thee, Lord.”

They stood there for a while. Mr. Wolfe didn’t know what to do; he touched his hat and watched a few people walk by.

“Yes, Mr. Wolfe.” Mr. Goad opened his eyes. “The Lord’s will has been revealed to me. I’ll appoint you to be my counsel.”

“Me… me?”

“Yes. The job might be intimidating; it’s not for the faint-hearted. May the Lord give you strength.”

“Why, yes! Yes! Mr. Goad, thank you for trusting me with such an important job!”

“It’s a calling.”

“Yes, such an important calling! I’m honored! Oh, what about Mrs. Donker?”

“What about her?”

“She’s the one giving the names.” Mr. Wolfe raised his hands. “But I happen to agree with all her inspirations.”

“Yes, yes, she’s a faithful Christian. You can work with her.”

“And Mrs. Fowler. They were big supporters of your big decision six years ago. What pure souls they are.”

“Indeed. Now, come to my worship room this afternoon, let’s discuss more names.”

“Will do, sir.”

Mr. Goad and Mr. Wolfe decided on ten names. It was a big number, yes, but it was better be safe than sorry. The next morning, no corpses were found. They had another ten names. Again, a peaceful morning.

“Have we caught the demon?” Mr. Goad asked.

“Possibly, but if we’re as careful and smart as my old mama, we know the demon can transform into another form when we don’t notice.”

“We should keep finding more names then.”

“I think so.”

Mr. Goad frowned. “At this rate, the village will be empty pretty soon.”

Mr. Wolfe scratched his head. “Oh.”

“Hmm.”

“Should we stop then?”

Mr. Goad touched his goatee. “No. We can’t let the demon win. Even if that means some sacrifice has to be made.”

“I see.”

Someone knocked on the door. “C’mon in. Oh, why, Mrs. Raven!” Mr. Goad rushed to welcome her with a smile that couldn’t be bigger and sweeter. “Why do I have the honor of your visit?” He held her hand.

“Ah, all the blood!” She sat down, touched her forehead, and closed her eyes. “When is it going to stop?”

“Now, the demon is cunning, we mustn’t let it get away. Even if that means some sacrifice has to be made.” Mr. Wolfe nodded with satisfaction, then looked at Mr. Goad for approval. And approval he got.

But Mrs. Raven stared at Mr. Wolfe with an intensity that could ignite a fire. “Such cruelty, such nonsense! I wouldn’t hear any of it! How dare you, Mr. Wolfe? Who are you to think a merciful God would grant you the right to do such things? A God who feeds the hungry, gives water to the thirsty, welcomes the strangers, clothes the naked, cares for the sick, and visits those imprisoned? How dare you?”

“I… No, I mean…” Mr. Wolfe’s face turned white.

“Dear Mrs. Raven, you shouldn’t let such worries pollute your purity. It’s a man’s job. A woman with such rare beauty shouldn’t be mad like this.”

“I’d be mad at whatever I see fit. Now, I came here to negotiate.”

“Negotiate?”

“I’ve learned something about demons and monsters myself. They ain’t capable of transforming into children. They might possess children, maybe, like what you claimed six years ago. But they can’t transform into children. So I forbid you to send any children to prison. Not to mention sending them to their end.”

“They ain’t capable?” Mr. Wolfe said.

“They ain’t!” She yelled.

Right, okay, they ain’t.”

“Also, their parents must be spared. At least their mothers.”

“Why?” Mr. Goad asked.

“Who would care for the children then? You?”

“Right.”

“And I forbid you to send more people to their end just because they’re not local.”

“But they’re evil. They take away villagers’ jobs and make them starve.” Mr. Goad frowned.

“That’s just nonsense! They’re doing the jobs the villagers are too proud to do. Finally, if they were children six years ago, they should be spared too.” 

Mr. Wolfe and Mr. Goad looked at each other.

“These poor kids have been traumatized ever since, thanks to you, Mr. Goad. Has anyone cared about their pain? No! The whole village cheered for it! Those who cheered must answer to the Lord for their crime! And now you want to murder them too? Is God even just anymore?”

“Mrs. Raven, this is just not right. What if they do horrible things now?” Mr. Wolfe asked.

“Then they’ll be judged by their crimes, not by your ‘inspiration.’”

“I don’t think so. This ain’t right. You might as well ask us to spare everyone! That ain’t gonna happen.” Mr. Wolfe shook his head.

Mrs. Raven burst into tears. “Mr. Wolfe! You insult the merciful Lord! You insult me! I won’t accept any of it! I won’t!”

“Mrs. Raven, be reasonable!” Mr. Wolfe raised his voice. “A woman!”

“A woman!? You feel inferior in front of a woman smarter than you? You feel threatened?”

“I’ll tell you what you should feel now, woman!” Mr. Wolfe shouted.

“Okay, alright, alright,” Mr. Goad said. “Mrs. Raven, we’ll think about it, alright? I promise we’ll think about it.”

“I want his apology!”

“In your dream!” Mr. Wolfe hissed.

“The insult!” She shed more tears. Looking at her tears, Mr. Goad shook his head. “Oh… For heaven’s sake, Mr. Wolfe, just apologize.”

“What? Why?”

“Mr. Wolfe. She’s a delicate flower.”

“…sorry, Mrs. Raven,” He murmured.

After she left, Mr. Goad nodded. “Mr. Wolfe, you have earned more trust in me now. A woman should know her place. Just don’t say it out loud to her.”

“Winning your trust is always my only goal.” Mr. Wolfe bowed to him.

* * *

For a week, only the machine was doing the job. Ten people at a time.

Though there were no more corpses in the morning, Mrs. Raven’s proposal caused almost the same level of disturbance and quarrels. It didn’t help her that more and more men became upset because they couldn’t get her to give them enough attention. They had had enough of her “No, thank you” or “No.” Many of them were married, sure, but that didn’t justify her rejection. After all, they were very nice to her, she should know she was obligated to reciprocate, they claimed.

Someone knocked on Mr. Goad’s door. A group of men and women, including Mrs. Donker and Mrs. Fowler, came in.

“What’s the matter?” Mr. Goad asked.

“Mr. Goad, have you thought about the unthinkable?” Mrs. Donker said.

“What do you mean?”

“Could it be Mrs. Raven?”

People around her looked at each other and nodded.

“Why?”

“Well, first of all, a beautiful woman like her shouldn’t be trusted.”

“I agree!” Mrs. Fowler said.

“Not to mention the idea of sparing everyone! She must be working with the demon! Or she’s the demon herself!” Some men shouted.

“Yes! She’s the demon!” Others chimed in.

Mr. Wolfe slapped his own forehead. “Oh no, that’s right!”

“What’s right?” Mr. Goad asked.

“If I know what my mama taught me, she’s no shape-shifter or skinwalker; they prefer to mingle with the crowd rather than turn into someone with such a prominent appearance. The smell ain’t right too. She’s not the demon—but a witch who works with them!” He yelled the last part. Pausing for a few seconds to enjoy being the center of attention, he continued, “We should do the water test!”

“Water test! We need to kill the witch!” Mrs. Donker shouted.

“We need to kill her! Swim the witch!” Mrs. Fowler followed.

“Swim the witch! Kill the witch!” Everyone chanted. “Swim the witch! Kill the witch!”

Mr. Goad nodded. “Valuable information, valuable indeed! Let’s swim a witch!”

Mrs. Raven was brought to the river. All the men who were rejected by her before were staring at her, smirking.

“Mrs. Raven,” Mr. Goad announced with authority. “You came to my worship place the other night and made some outrageous proposals. I was fooled by you then. But God will not be mocked. You are to be tied up and sunk into the river. If you float, you’re the witch because the water of baptism rejects you. If you sink, you’re innocent. May God have mercy on your evil soul.”

“You!” Mrs. Raven roared. “All you people! I negotiated to spare the lives of so many of you! And this is how you repay me? Is this what your God teaches you? Hypocrisy? Greed for power? Murder? Where’s love and turning the other cheek?”

“One has only so many cheeks to turn,” one man said coldly.

She was stripped to her undergarments. Many men were gawking, commenting, and mocking. Then they tied her up and fastened a rope around her waist.

Mr. Wolfe sneered at her. “We’ll see if you’re a witch.” He pushed her into the water.

She sank.
