Short Story - Horror

Chapter 1

People said he had lived in that little hut alone for thirty years. Maybe longer. In the late 1800s, especially in a small town like this one, it was fertile ground for all kinds of rumors to swarm like termites in spring. Some said he suffered from mental illness. Some said he was possessed. Some said he had killed someone. A fugitive.

It wasn’t easy to get to the hut from the town. You would have to walk along a long and narrow trail, which almost no one used. The weeds were almost as tall as a grown man, and the trees almost totally blocked the sunlight. Even the most enthusiastic birders who loved the exceptionally rare blue jays with green wings to a feverish degree would think twice before taking the path to the oak tree.

Yes, the oak. About a quarter-mile from the hut, toward the hilltop, stood a big old oak tree. It was at least four centuries old. The townspeople avoided even looking at it if they could. They called it “the lady in the black snood.” Rumors had it that a ghost had claimed it as her home forever, and if any soul provoked the spirit, their neck would be broken like a chicken in the slaughterhouse.

The man who lived in the little hut never talked to anyone. On rare occasions, he did show up in town. Whenever he did, it was always deep at night. He would hide his face with his hat and black trench coat, only letting his eyes make contact with the outside world. Of those who had seen his eyes, some said they were full of sorrow mixed with shame, some said they showed regret and fear. They all agreed: It seemed as if someone was always behind him and he couldn’t escape.
