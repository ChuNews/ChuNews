Game of the Night (1 of 4)

Mark Chu October 18, 2026

Short Story - Fantasy / Horror

Chapter 1

It was an especially dark night, the moon was hidden behind a thick cloud. A middle-aged man walked through the woods, carrying a torch. He had a bushy beard and a bald head. He was mumbling to himself: “I should have got two more silver coins for that. After all, if someone has to cheat to be rich, it should be me…”

Suddenly, he heard a strange noise. It was brief and soft like leaves rustling, but something about it got him curious. “What is it?” he murmured.

Then he noticed how quiet it was. “Someone’s there?”

The quietness became eerie now, like a thick cloud pouring down on his head. “Show thyself!”

He didn’t even have time to blink, a shadow jumped on him. He fell without making a sound.

* * *

 “You found him this morning?”

The next day just a little before noon, three men were standing around the dead man’s body. The one in the middle who spoke was Mr. Goad, a short old man with a long face. His mustache and goatee were white as snow, and his small eyes always looked at people with skepticism.

“Yes sir,” the man on his left said. “This morning I was on my way to the market, and he was lying there face down. He must have seen something that didn’t agree with him. Poor fella.”

“And this is the fourth death this week. All without a good reason: no injury, no sign of fights.” Mr. Goad lightly hit the body with his black heavy cane.

“That must be a vampire,” the man on Mr. Goad’s right said. “He was killed by a vampire.”

“Beg your pardon?” Mr. Goad frowned.

“All his blood was sucked dry! Don’t you see?” His trembling finger pointed at the body. “Look at his neck, aren’t there two holes?”

Mr. Goad and the first man took a closer look at the body. “He doesn’t look dry to me,” the first man said, moving the corpse’s head around to check his neck. “And I don’t see no holes.”

“Then what is that?” The second man pointed at a small red spot on the body’s right side of the neck.

“That’s just a red spot… a rash?” the first man said.

“Well, that’s a sign of a vampire’s attack!” The second man insisted.

“And you know that’s a sign of a vampire’s attack because…” the first man asked.

“Because he was killed by a vampire!”

The first man scratched his head. “I’m not sure…”

“This was no vampire,” Mr. Goad sniffed. “I’ve seen so many cases like this I lose count. I can smell them from a mile away.”

“What is it, then?” the first man asked.

Mr. Goad stroked his goatee. “Evil spirit, son. Evil spirit. I’ve met this kind before, during the day it hid in caves or trees, and at night it’ll come out, pick an unfortunate soul and take it away from the rightful owner just for fun.”

The second man gulped. “That’s nothing nicer than a vampire. Not a bit.”

“I’ve sent countless possessed peasants to jail or the guillotine. I know how to take matters like this into my own hands. You watch, sons; you just watch.” He patted them both on their shoulders. “We’ve got ourselves a war... but I’m going to put an end to it. By putting a rope around the evil spirit’s neck.”

“Mr. Goad, you don’t think this to be the wrath from God, do you?” the first man asked.

“Why on earth do you think that?” Mr. Goad hissed.

“That thing we did a few years ago…”

Mr. Goad cleared his throat. “Nonsense. This is no wrath from God. We did nothing wrong. This is an evil spirit.”

“What should we do?” the second man asked.

“Let’s go tell the village head, tell him to get everyone together.” Mr. Goad raised his head.

* * *

This tiny village had less than two hundred people. The village head, Mr. Hipol, was a big man in his late forties. For some reason his face was always red, and he perspired a lot, even when he wasn’t moving around. He rarely made a decision. In his own words, he liked to wait till things “straighten themselves out.”

Mr. Hipol gathered all the villagers for a meeting in the village house. The news already spread like fire. Though it was a particularly warm day, everyone felt a chill breath down their necks, making them shiver. “What happened?”

“A man was killed last night!”

“What, another one!?”

“They said these people were all killed by werewolves!”

“No, it was zombies, they were eaten alive!”

“Their skulls were crushed and brains spilled all over!”

“Mr. Hipol, what should we do? Should we run now? Where?” Mrs. Donker shouted.

Mrs. Donker was in her thirties. She was a small woman with pale skin, but her voice was as loud as her fire-red hair.

“I… I don’t…” Mr. Hipol stuttered and shook his head hard.

“No need to panic, my good people!” Mr. Goad yelled. “Have four people died mysteriously? Yes. Were they killed by some creature of monstrous nature? Indeed. Is the devil still among us? Definitely. Will it kill again? Most likely. But have I let any evil spirits and witches escape from justice? Never!” He waved his cane.

“What does it want anyway? Maybe if we give it what it wants it’ll go away!” Mrs. Donker said. Many people nodded.

“Now, now, my people, you can’t reason with the devil. It’s the enemy of all mankind and it wants to destroy every single soul on earth. That’s what it wants,” Mr. Goad said. “But never underestimate the power of faith and prayer. Here, let’s pray together to chastise the devil and resist its control. Repeat after me.” He put down his cane and raised his arms high, citing the scripture: “Submit yourselves, then, to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.”

Everyone repeated.

“There shall no evil befall thee. For he shall give his angels charge over thee.”

Repeated again.

“See, feel the Lord’s protection now?”

Silence.

Seeing people’s faces, he raised his cane. “Did I mention my cane has holy power too? It’s helped me so many times to chase down witches and monsters. Tonight I’m going to use my cane combined with the power our Lord gives me to protect us. Go home, or do whatever is necessary. But when the sun sets, lock your door and do not go out and about. I say: do not go out. Stay inside and pray like a good man. The spirit won’t be able to hurt you and I promise that’s true.”

“And you’ll hunt it down?” Mrs. Donker asked.

“As sure as I live and breathe.” Mr. Goad nodded.

After everyone left, Mr. Hipol asked Mr. Goad, “People will be safe, right?”

“I trust myself and I have the power. Now if you excuse me, I have a whole village of people to protect.” He knocked his cane on a table and proudly walked out of the door. 

* * *

The next day, another body was found near the forest. It was an old rich widow. Again, no sign of injury or struggle.

Everyone gathered at the village house again, yelling, crying, and praying. “Mr. Goad, where’s your power of protection?” Mrs. Donker shrieked.

Mr. Goad cleared his throat. “Well, didn’t I tell you not to go out and about? What was she doing out there after dark?”

“I thought she was blind and stayed home all the time?” Mr. Hipol said.

“So it’s her fault?” Mrs. Donker asked.

“As surely as there is a hell. I know the Lord is just. He won’t let bad things happen to good people.”

A tanned woman in her late twenties raised her hand. “Yes, Mrs. Fowler,” Mr. Goad said.

Mrs. Fowler was a hard-working farmer; one could always see her in the field with a tool in her hand. She said, “Won’t bad things sometimes happen to good people? My neighbor was a nice old lady and we all loved her dearly, but she died of a horrible disease nobody knew what.”

“Then God had a purpose for her. There’s a purpose for everything and a reason for everything that happens. Or, maybe she did some bad things without us knowing; didn’t you know a righteous man’s good deeds can’t save him once he commits a crime? No worries, as long as you do not surrender to any temptation, you will be fine.”

“Thank you, Mr. Goad, for your wisdom.” Mrs. Fowler put one hand on her chest.

“But the widow was blind and lived a quiet life…” Some murmured.

“We believe you, Mr. Goad!” Mrs. Fowler claimed loudly.

“I still don’t feel safe, Mr. Goad,” Mr. Hipol said, wiping sweat from his face.

“Do something then, aren’t you the village head?” Another guy said.

“No no, no one does anything but me. Only people with authority, with the key to the preshood, like myself, can deal with it.” Mr. Goad tapped his cane. “Now, we need to determine what exactly it is. It’s an evil spirit, that’s established, but of what kind?”

“Could it be a devil in a teen girl’s form, like before?” Mrs. Donker said. “She was especially deceiving!”

“We’ve cleansed the spirits of their power to possess people under my guidance already.” Mr. Goad touched his goatee. “But an evil spirit somehow manifested in a child’s form… that’s not unheard of.”

“Should we round up children?” Mr. Hipol asked.

Mr. Goad pondered for a while. He opened his mouth to speak, nodding, “Maybe we indeed…”

A woman’s scream from outside shocked everyone. People rushed out to investigate and saw the woman lying on the ground with her eyes closed. “Mrs. Raven! Are you alright?” People helped her to sit up.

“Mrs. Raven, did you see something?” “Mrs. Raven, did anybody try to hurt you?” “Mrs. Raven, let me help you!” People, especially men, eagerly expressed their concerns. It probably was because Mrs. Raven was an attractive woman. No one could truly tell how old she was; she never revealed her age. They guessed she was between twenty-five and thirty. Her long dark hair and exotic eyes made people want to get her attention. No one had ever seen Mr. Raven, who was supposedly traveling abroad on business and hadn't been home in years.

Mrs. Raven slowly opened her eyes and whispered, “Can I have some water?”

Five men rushed to fetch some water. After she drank a cup, Mrs. Fowler asked, “What happened, Mrs. Raven?”

“I saw a monster of evil nature…” Mrs. Raven trembled. “It went… that way.” She pointed at an abandoned hut about a quarter of a mile away.

Everyone looked at Mr. Goad. He nodded. A bunch of men grabbed whatever they could get and ran toward it. Soon, they carried a body out of the hut. It was a shoemaker in his thirties.

“What, in this broad daylight?” Mrs. Donker gasped.

“How come it didn’t hurt you, Mrs. Raven?” Mr. Owler asked. He was a short man with white hair. A skilled carpenter, he also helped design a corn mill and irrigation system using “woo-woo” knowledge, as villagers called it.

“Well, it must be getting more and more bloodthirsty, but your purity and faith chased it away.” Mr. Goad patted Mrs. Raven’s hand.

“Thank you, Mr. Goad.” Mrs. Raven smiled.

“You’re welcome. Did you see what it looked like?”

“I couldn’t tell, but it might look like an old, ugly man.”

“I see, I see, valuable information.” Mr. Goad nodded. “But we still can’t totally rule out a spirit in a child’s form, the most deceiving kind, somehow manifests itself to…”

A tall man in his twenties interrupted. “What kind of evil spirit dares to show up when the sun is shining? Maybe it’s not an evil spirit? Maybe… a monster like a shape-shifter? A skinwalker? My old mama taught me they ain’t afraid of daylight when I was a kid…”

Mr. Goad hmphed. “Why, Mr. Wolfe, you think you know your supernatural creatures? Better than a Lord-appointed messenger like myself?”

A man with strong arms, Mr. Wolfe worked as a stonemason. He let out a nervous laugh. “No no, Mr. Goad, please, I didn’t mean to offend you. I just offered my humble opinion.”

“I know what I know, and if anyone wants to live, you better listen to me.”

Everyone mumbled, “Yes.”

“Mr. Goad, I feel your holy authority!” Mr. Wolfe quickly followed. “I feel it in my body. You’re a Holy Man.”

“Evil spirits, show thyself!” Mr. Goad shouted, raising both arms and looking at the sky. “Or I’d chase you down and destroy you with the authority given to me from the Lord!”

“Now, Mr. Goad, I think we should take a step back and see this whole thing from a different perspective…” Mr. Owler said.

“Devil, begone!” Mr. Goad waved his fist.

“Devil, begone!” Mrs. Fowler and Mrs. Donker followed. Everyone followed as well.

“Wait, we need to be careful…” But Mr. Owler’s voice was drowned out by the crowd’s chanting.
