Game of the Night (2 of 4)

Mark Chu October 18, 2026

Short Story - Fantasy / Horror

Chapter 2

When two more bodies were found the next day, Mr. Goad ordered the guillotine to be brought out and displayed at the plaza in front of the village house. “It was revealed to me last night by the Holy Spirit that the evil spirit is able to turn itself into human form. It’s time to catch and execute the evil among us!” He loudly claimed.

“The evil spirit is among us?” Mr. Hipol said.

“Just like six years ago. Only at that time, they possessed humans instead of becoming one.”

“How did you know it’s not possessing this time?” Mr. Hipol asked.

Mr. Goad scornfully said, “As I mentioned, because six years ago we cleansed up all the possessive evil spirits, of course. I cleansed them all up.”

“By imprisoning children and shedding innocent blood.” Mr. Olwer said.

“Nonsense!” Mr. Goad interrupted, his cane hit on the guillotine so loudly that everyone was startled. “Every. Single. One. Of. Them. Was. Justly. Imprisoned. Or. Executed!! They were weak-minded; they allowed the devilish lust, the dirty desire for carnal knowledge to pollute their thoughts, surrendering themselves to temptation! As true as God lives, I was right and the whole village cheered for me! The whole village! Every single villager!”

“When will we have peace?” Mrs. Donker cried.

“I just think our merciful and all-powerful Lord wouldn’t want us to make more mistakes if we can prevent it, right?” Mr. Owler said.

“That’s right! That’s why we must find the spirit and kill it now!” Mrs. Donker said.

“No! That’s not what I meant…” Mr. Owler tried to be louder.

“We need to do something quick!” Mrs. Fowler said.

“Indeed, rightfully so!” Mr. Goad cleared his throat and announced, “From now on, if you see anyone acting suspicious, come to me! I’ll be in my worship room praying.”

* * *

In less than one hour, someone knocked on Mr. Goad’s door.

“C’mon in. Oh, Mr. Ante, what can I help you with today?”

Mr. Ante was a hatter with a big mustache. “I believe I saw the evil spirit, sir. See, last night I was walking down the street minding my own business, and I think I saw the cook from that big house, you know? I don’t know, he just looked guilty, like the way he walked wasn’t like, the way a God-fearing guy should walk, you know?”

“The cook, huh? Hmm, interesting.”

“Yeah, yeah. And see, he isn’t local and he misses church.” Mr. Ante shrugged with open palms. “That’s suspicious enough, no?”

“True, true! Valuable information, Mr. Ante! Valuable, indeed.”

Two hours later, the cook was dragged to the public and had his head cut off, amid people’s shouting and cursing. Mr. Goad grinned and nodded with approval. “Well done. Now we shall live in peace.”

People cheered. Mr. Owler stood away from the crowd, shaking his head.

* * *

It wasn’t comforting at all when the villagers found three more bodies in the woods the very next morning. They were near the lake. No blood, no injury. Only some mud on their bodies and faces.

“How… how many evil spirits are there?” Mr. Wolfe asked, trembling.

“Mr. Goad, what should we do?” Mrs. Donker pleaded.

Mr. Goad stared at the guillotine for a moment. “Mr. Ante, did you see what you thought you saw that night?”

“Sure did!”

“What did you see?”

“The cook walked on the street, acting all suspicious.”

“And what was the crime again?”

“The… the way he walked. Suspicious and all that. And not attending church. Oh, and he was not from here, see.”

“So, what were you doing when seeing him?”

“Walked… walked down the street.”

“At night?”

Mr. Ante’s eyes widened. “Hey, hey now…”  

People turned to stare at him.

“Wicked devil, you think you could fool a prophet twice!?” Mr. Goad shouted with unmistakable authority.

“It’s him! It’s him!” Everyone shouted.

Mr. Ante’s head was cut off.

“Now, go home and pray,” Mr. Goad ordered. “So our Lord have mercy on your souls.”

“Praise the Lord!” Mrs. Donker said.

“We shall have peace now, thank you, Mr. Goad.” Mrs. Fowler said.

“I should go home and rest, have a long overdue good sleep.” Mr. Hipol wiped his face.

Mr. Owler scanned the crowd. Mr. Wolfe was cleaning his shoes while begging people around to pray with him.

* * *

That night, Mr. Goad studied scriptures with good effort. His reading skills were somehow limited, but enough to interpret the Holy Bible, or at least say something about it. He was digging deep into the book, trying to find more authority to claim.

Someone knocked on the door. “C’mon in.”

It was Mrs. Donker. “Mr. Goad, I trust you, our prophet, with all my heart. I don’t doubt you a bit. You’re God’s messenger, that’s as true as day.”

“…But?”

“I mean, what if… what if Mr. Ante wasn’t the only evil spirit? Or, what if the evil spirit transformed into another person before Mr. Ante was beheaded?”

“Hmm. Why do you think that?”

“I’m just afraid tomorrow morning we’ll find more bodies. In case we do, I talked to some other villagers, and we all agreed the shepherd with the big nose could be it.”

“Why?”

“Did you know when his wife died, he didn’t even cry? Mrs. Fowler even swore that she saw him smile once.”

“No love in his heart, yes, that could be it.”

“Praise the Lord.”

“Now, Mrs. Donker,” Mr. Goad said, looking around before holding her hand. “You’re safe with me.”

“Ah, Mr. Goad. You’re too lovely.”

* * *

The next morning, people searched around and found no dead bodies. They danced and sang, hailing Mr. Goad as a hero. “It was Mr. Ante!” “Of course! He never sat right with me!”

But just half an hour after sunset, a woman’s scream shook everyone. Right next to a well was the wife of a blacksmith. Though in her forties, she was as strong as her husband. Yet she obviously hadn’t even put up a fight.

“What happened? Didn’t you say all is well now? I just saw her ten minutes ago! Now she’s dead!” Mrs. Fowler asked Mr. Goad.

“I… uh…”

“It’s Mr. Wolfe!” A man shouted. Everyone turned to the voice.

It was Mr. Owler.

Mr. Wolfe’s face turned white. “No… please… I beg everyone to pray with me every day!” He looked as though he might puke at any second. “I don’t even eat pork! I don’t even know what’s going on. Are there evil spirits? A shape-shifter? A skinwalker? My old mama taught me they ain’t afraid of daylight when I was a kid…”

“Where were you just five minutes ago?” Mr. Owler questioned.

“I went to take a piss and shit.”

“He’s about to shit himself again,” some laughed.

“How did you know it was him?” Mr. Hipol asked.

“The three bodies found near the lake had mud on them. I saw Mr. Wolfe cleaning mud off his shoes!”

Mrs. Raven, who stood and observed quietly, let out a sigh. Though soft, half of the men turned to her. “What’s the matter, Mrs. Raven?”

“The blacksmith’s wife was a good friend of mine. I still owe her two coins. She was such a nice and strong woman.”

“Aye.”

“Everybody, listen! I’m sure it’s Mr. Wolfe!” Mr. Owler said.

Mr. Wolfe’s face was now grey. He whimpered and was about to bawl. “Please… Have mercy… I pray every day… Mrs. Donker, you know me well, speak for me!”

“No, Mr. Wolfe ain’t it. I don’t see it.” Mrs. Donker shook her head. “It’s someone else. I know.”

“Nah. Mr. Wolfe had no clue; he even asked how many evil spirits were there,” Mrs. Fowler added.

People mocked Mr. Owler. “You and your woo-woo knowledge are no good at finding an evil spirit!” “The woo-woo knowledge is no good at anything!”

“It’s the shepherd!” Mrs. Donker claimed, pointing a finger at him. “I knew it all along!”

“I agree!” Mrs. Fowler followed. “Kill him!”

“Right! Right, the one without love in heart, how could I forget?” Mr. Goad cried.

“You guys are mistaken!” Mr. Owler yelled.

“Help!” The shepherd cried.

The shepherd’s head was cut off.

That night, no one slept with their eyes closed.

* * *

Finally, the morning came. People went out to search for bodies. They didn’t find any. The next two days were the same.

“So… It really was the shepherd?” People were more cautious, looking at each other.

“It was the shepherd! I was right!” Mrs. Donker gladly claimed.

“And I too!” Mrs. Fowler followed.

“So glad this is over,” Mrs. Raven was panting, about to faint. “I can’t stand all the deaths and blood. Too cruel.”

At least six men stood by her side, comforting her. “It’s over now, don’t be afraid,” a man said.

“Anyone who wants to hurt you will answer to my fist first.” The man saying that flexed his fist and bicep while looking at her heaving chest.

* * *

That night, believing it was safe now, Mr. Wolfe had Mr. Doggs, an old brewer, walk with him across the village to collect some stones. Mr. Doggs was a notorious gossip; all the stories, real or fake, would be broadcast by him.

“Mr. Doggs, I’ve heard Mrs. Donker and Mr. Goad know each other a little too well?”

“Sheesh!” Mr. Doggs covered his mouth and looked around. “There are things not supposed to be said out loud!”

“Oh, I’m sorry, truly sorry… I won’t say a word.”

“Not out loud, just whisper.”

“Oh.”

“You heard right.”

“I was right?”

“Yeah.”

Mr. Wolfe waited for ten seconds. “… that’s it?”

Mr. Doggs shrugged. “Mr. Goad is a powerful man.”

“Of course. Is Mrs. Donker the only…”

“Sheesh!” He covered his mouth again.

“Okay, I’m not sure what is okay and what is not…” He muffled.

“No! You hear that?”

“Hmm?”

They both looked around. “There…” Mr. Wolfe’s face turned white. He whispered, “There seems to be… some breathing…”

Suddenly, a shadow jumped over their heads, screeching and laughing before it disappeared. “Ah!” They both screamed.

“Run!” Mr. Wolfe grabbed Mr. Doggs’s arm and ran for their lives.

* * *

Imagine the shock and discouragement when the villagers found two more bodies the next morning.

They were Mr. Hipol and Mr. Owler.
