Katy (4 of 4)

Mark Chu November 7, 2026

Short Story - Horror

Chapter 4

Katy’s body remained in the makeshift quarters, the county morgue, for many days. People were too scared even to look at her. She was a ghost. A walking dead. Cursed and condemned. Even her own mother, who came to identify the body, left immediately without looking back, requesting that her daughter not be buried near the family.

A reporter named Fred, who was appalled by the situation, appealed for her body to be properly cleaned up by a mortician. The sawdust was brushed away from her hair, face, and clothing before burial. She rests forever under her favorite oak tree. No tombstone. Just a rock with her name carved on it. No one dared to go near it.

At Norman’s funeral, George broke down. “I’m sorry…I’m sorry…” He wept and wept. “Please, I shall pay for my crime… I shall pay…”

Madam Burgess went to the Police Chief and questioned his judgment to show a corpse to her son, but the Police Chief was puzzled and denied even meeting him. He was in his office all night, he claimed. Katy’s body was sent to the morgue from the makeshift operation room before sunset.

George got sick and was bedridden for two months. He almost died. When he finally came to, he built himself a little hut near the oak tree and lived there, cutting off all contact with the outside world. His mother begged in tears, asking him to reconsider, to come home. He simply kept on saying that it was the only way to pay the debt.

Thirty-four years later, a group of reckless travelers from outside the town didn’t listen to locals’ warnings and ventured onto the trail. They found George’s body kneeling, his head bowed, with a piece of brown cloth in hand. The brown color came from old blood. They believed George had been dead for about two days.

In front of his kneeling body was a tiny window. The oak tree was in full view.

When people carried out George’s body, they saw a blue jay with green wings flying above them. It then descended to the ground under the oak tree and perched on Katy’s grave.

More than a century had passed. Truth became legend, stories became myths. Lives were forgotten and memories faded away. Katy’s tale was remembered no more.

Until two people—one a reporter, another a kind-hearted saloonist—heard about the story in 1997. They served as benefactors and paid to have a marker placed at Katy’s grave. On the marker was inscribed:

Seduced and pregnant by her father's friend.

Unwed, she died from abortion, her only choice.

Abandoned in life and death by family.

With but a single rose from her mother.

Buried only through the kindness of unknown benefactors.

Died February, 1875, age 21.

Victim of an unforgiving society

Have mercy on us.*

* This inscription can be found on a marker in Elmwood Cemetery, Memphis, Tennessee, in the name of Kate McCormick. But the “rose-from-her-mother” line very likely didn’t happen, and the benefactors’ identities were actually known.
